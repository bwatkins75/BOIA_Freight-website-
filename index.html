Platform overview
Tech sheet 01

One system that carries a load from the moment it's quoted to the moment it's paid — replacing the handoffs between a TMS, a scanning app, a fuel portal, a spreadsheet and accounting.

What it runs

  • Loads and dispatch — booking, rating, assignment, dispatch board
  • Live tracking — position and ETA from your existing ELD
  • Document capture — BOL, POD, lumper receipts, compliance photos
  • Billing and settlement — invoice packets, payment tracking
  • Fuel and cost — card data matched to the load that burned it
  • Maintenance, light — mileage alerts, cost allocated per load
  • Customer and driver portals — browser-based, no app install

What it replaces

  • The scanning app and the shared drive of loose PDFs
  • The spreadsheet holding rates, margin and detention
  • Manual rekeying between dispatch and accounting
  • Chasing drivers for paperwork after the fact
  • Quarterly cost-per-mile guesswork

What stays yours

  • Your ELD provider and contract
  • Your fuel card program and pricing
  • Your accounting system, if you want to keep it

How it's built

  • Modular — every area toggles on or off, so the screen only shows what you use
  • Per-truck pricing — unlimited logins, so nobody shares credentials to see a number
  • Browser-based — office, tablet in the cab, phone at the dock
  • Your data stays yours — exportable, and it leaves with you if you ever go

Getting started
Tech sheet 03

You don't stop running freight to switch systems. The first loads move in parallel with whatever you use today, and the old system doesn't go dark until you say so.

The first 30 days

WeekWhat happens
Week 1Setup and configuration. Trucks, drivers, customers and lanes loaded. Modules you don't need are switched off.
Week 2Connections made — ELD, fuel card, accounting export. Your document templates and invoice format matched.
Week 3Live on a slice. A few trucks run real loads end to end while everything else stays where it is.
Week 4Full cutover. Remaining trucks moved over, the old system kept read-only for reference.

What we need from you

  • Truck, trailer and driver list
  • Customer list with billing terms
  • ELD and fuel card account access
  • A sample invoice and rate confirmation
  • One person who knows how the office actually runs

Training

  • Dispatch — about two hours, most of it hands on
  • Office and billing — about an hour
  • Drivers — a few minutes; the app walks them through it
  • Support during cutover, on the phone, same people who set it up

If it doesn't fit

  • Your data exports in full at any point
  • Nothing is locked to a long contract during the first cycle

Where the return comes from
Tech sheet 04

The platform doesn't earn its keep by being cheaper than what you have. It earns it by moving four line items you're already living with — and by letting the same people run more trucks.

The four line items

Line itemWhat changes
Unbilled detentionDock time is timestamped by geofence, not remembered. Billable wait ends up on the invoice instead of being written off.
Days to get paidThe invoice packet assembles at delivery and goes out the same day, complete. Fewer rejections, shorter aging.
Loads taken at a lossReal cost per mile — fuel receipts, maintenance, driver pay — is known before you accept, not after the quarter closes.
Office hours per loadRekeying between five systems stops. That time either goes back to the day or gets spent on more trucks.

Check it against your own numbers

  • Pull last month's loads. How many had dock wait over two hours, and how many of those were billed?
  • What's your average days-to-pay, and how many invoices came back for a missing document?
  • How many loads last quarter came in under your true cost per mile once fuel and maintenance were counted?
  • How many hours a week does the office spend moving information between systems?

How the cost scales

  • Per truck, not per seat — the price follows the fleet, which is the thing that actually grows
  • Unlimited logins — adding a dispatcher or giving the owner access costs nothing
  • Modules off — you're not carrying the price of features you don't run

The return doesn't need to be dramatic to be real. One properly billed detention charge a week covers what a truck costs to run on the platform.
